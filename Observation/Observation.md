# Direct Observation – Vani Tuition Centre System


**Observed Environment:** Regular tuition centre operations  
**Observed Activities:** Student registration, attendance marking, assignment handling, marks entry, payment-related administration, and parent communication  
**Purpose:** To study the actual working environment of the tuition centre and validate the information collected through the structured interview.

## 2. Observation Protocol

The observation focused on the following operational areas:

1. How student and teacher records are created, stored, and retrieved.
2. How attendance is recorded during class sessions.
3. How assignments are distributed, collected, and tracked.
4. How marks are recorded and communicated to students and parents.
5. How payments are tracked and overdue payments are identified.
6. How parents are contacted regarding attendance, payments, meetings, and announcements.
7. How long it takes staff to retrieve records and prepare reports.
8. Whether information is stored centrally or across separate registers, files, and spreadsheets.

## 3. Method

Direct observation was carried out during regular tuition centre operations. Activities such as student registration, attendance marking, assignment handling, marks entry, and parent communication were carefully observed.

The purpose was to compare actual day-to-day practices with the information obtained during the structured interview and identify operational inefficiencies that may not be fully visible through interview responses alone.

## 4. Field Notes and Observations

- Attendance was recorded manually during each class session.
- Student and teacher information was stored in physical registers and spreadsheets.
- Assignment distribution and collection required significant manual coordination.
- Marks were recorded manually before being shared with students and parents.
- Parent notifications were sent individually, leading to delays and inefficiency.
- Payment information was maintained manually, making overdue-payment tracking difficult.
- Reporting and information retrieval required manual searching and compilation.
- The different operational processes were not connected through a centralised digital system.

## 5. Findings

- Manual processes increased the risk of data-entry errors.
- Administrative workload was high because staff repeatedly performed the same tasks.
- Reporting and information retrieval were time-consuming.
- The absence of centralised data storage reduced efficiency and consistency.
- Physical records were vulnerable to loss, damage, and human error.
- Parent communication depended on individual calls or messages and could be delayed or missed.
- The existing process was difficult to scale as the number of students increased.

## 6. Existing Workflow Identified

### 6.1 Student and Teacher Record Management

Student and teacher records were maintained using physical registers, paper files, and spreadsheets. Staff had to search these sources manually when retrieving or updating information.

### 6.2 Attendance Management

Attendance was marked by a teacher or administrator using a paper-based class register. No central digital backup or automated reporting process was available.

### 6.3 Assignment Management

Assignments were printed or handwritten and distributed during classes. Students returned submissions physically, and there was no digital record of who had received, submitted, or missed an assignment.

### 6.4 Marks Management

Teachers recorded marks manually in registers. Results were shared verbally or using written mark sheets. Parents generally depended on students to receive this information.

### 6.5 Parent Communication

Parents were contacted through individual phone calls or manually written messages. There was no automated communication process for attendance, overdue payments, parent meetings, or general announcements.

### 6.6 Payment Tracking

Payment information was maintained in handwritten ledgers. There was no automated method to identify overdue payments or send reminders to parents.


## 9. Observation Outcome

The observation confirmed the issues identified during the structured interview and provided additional evidence of operational inefficiencies in the existing tuition centre processes. These findings support the need for the EduVani system as a centralised web-based platform for record management, QR attendance, assignments, marks, payments, notifications, reports, and AI learning support.
